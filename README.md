# Soda Experiment on Taste
This experiment investigates how two factors, cup material and presence of ice, influence the perceived taste of soda. A full factorial design with blocking was used, participants rated different drink conditions to uncover how sensory elements like temperature and texture affect satisfaction.

## Background and Motivation
This experiment was designed to explore how small sensory details—like cup material and the presence of ice—can affect how people perceive the taste of a drink. With the beverage industry rapidly shifting in response to changing health trends and consumer preferences, there's a growing interest in how presentation affects enjoyment. As traditional soft drinks lose popularity (Suhag, 2017), brands are searching for new ways to connect with health-conscious customers, and sometimes that comes down to more than just the ingredients.

Rather than changing the drink itself, there's an opportunity to tweak the experience around it. One previous study (Lefebvre, 2019) showed that even virtual images of drinks in different containers could shift consumer perception. Building on that idea, this study aimed to test real-life conditions to see if factors like cup material and ice influence the way people actually taste a drink.

## Objective
The goal was to find out whether the material of the cup (glass, plastic, or metal) and the presence or absence of ice impact how participants rate the taste of a soft drink. Since these factors can change things like temperature, texture, and even expectations, the experiment was set up to isolate and observe their effects.

## Experimental Setup
A 2x3 factorial design was used with blocking by participant. The two factors tested were:

Ice: Yes / No

Cup Material: Glass, Plastic (polystyrene), Metal (stainless steel)

Each participant tried all six combinations in a random order. There were six participants, so a total of 36 observations were collected. Blocking by participant helped control for differences in individual taste preferences.

To keep conditions consistent:

All drinks were poured from the same soda, held at room temperature.

Ice (if present) was added before pouring.

The same three cups were reused across trials with proper cleaning in between.

Participants cleansed their palate with water between drinks.

## Data Collected
Each observation recorded:

Taste score (0–10)

Ice condition (Yes/No)

Cup material (Glass/Plastic/Metal)

Participant Number (used as block)

## Statistical Methods
A series of linear models were built to evaluate how the factors and their interactions affected taste scores. The full model included both factors and blocking, while several null models removed specific effects to test their significance. Partial F-tests (via ANOVA) were used to compare the models and identify which variables significantly contributed to the results.

Assumptions for the analysis included:

Normal distribution of residuals

Equal variances across groups

No unexplained structure in the data

Given the small sample size, no prior power analysis was done. Instead, a post-hoc power analysis will be conducted based on the results to evaluate effect sizes.

## Technical Notes
The only issue that came up was related to temperature control. Since the experiment was done indoors during winter, the room temperature dropped over time, triggering the heater to turn on. That shift may have influenced the starting temperature of some drinks. Aside from that, the experiment ran smoothly without any major complications.
